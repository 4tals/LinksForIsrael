# New Olim Guide — Your First 90 Days in Israel

Making Aliyah is exciting — and comes with a fair amount of bureaucracy. This guide lays out the most important practical steps for the first 90 days after landing, alongside the verified organizations and hotlines that will support you for free.

Full list of Aliyah, absorption, employment and lone-soldier organizations: **[English Aliyah & Olim Directory](/en?cat=olim)** | **[Hebrew Aliyah & Absorption section](/olim)**.

---

## One hotline to save in your phone

- **Ministry of Aliyah and Integration hotline:** **`*2994`** (or `03-9733333`) — available Sunday–Thursday (08:00–18:00) in 7 languages: Hebrew, English, Russian, French, Spanish, Portuguese and Amharic.

---

## Weeks 1–2: Right after landing

1. **Teudat Oleh and temporary ID (at Ben Gurion Airport or the Ministry of Aliyah office):**
   Most olim receive their Teudat Oleh, ID number, initial health-fund registration and the first Sal Klita (absorption basket) payment at the airport. Keep a digital copy of every document.
2. **Open an Israeli bank account:**
   Go to a bank branch with your passport, Teudat Oleh and ID. Ask for an **account confirmation letter (Ishur Nihul Cheshbon)** — it is required to receive the remaining **Sal Klita** payments directly to your account.
3. **Complete your health-fund registration (Clalit / Maccabi / Meuhedet / Leumit):**
   New olim are exempt from health insurance contributions in the first months (subject to National Insurance eligibility). Make sure you have a membership card and access to the app for booking appointments.
4. **Israeli phone line + the MyVisit app:**
   Appointments with the Population Authority (biometric ID / passport), the Ministry of Aliyah and the Licensing Office are mostly booked through `MyVisit` or `gov.il`.

## Months 1–2: Ulpan, housing and ongoing rights

- **Hebrew Ulpan (free or by voucher):**
  New olim are entitled to a free Ulpan Aleph (in the standard track or through the **Ulpan voucher** program for private/online study). Contact your absorption counselor at the Ministry of Aliyah and Integration.
- **Arnona (municipal tax) discount (up to 90% in the first year):**
  Bring your rental contract and Teudat Oleh to the municipality or its website — olim are entitled to a significant Arnona discount for 12 months during the first two years after Aliyah (up to 100 sqm).
- **Rent assistance:**
  Once the Sal Klita period ends, many olim are entitled to monthly rent assistance (for several years, depending on family status).
- **Income tax credit points + 10-year exemption:**
  - **Extra tax credit points** on your salary for 3.5 years from the date of Aliyah (make sure your employer updates Form 101!).
  - **Tax exemption on foreign income** (new oleh / veteran returning resident benefits) — consulting an accountant who specializes in olim is recommended.
- **Converting a foreign driving license:**
  Olim with 5 or more years of driving experience can convert their license in a shortened process without a practical test (subject to an eye exam and an online form at the Ministry of Transport).

Full, up-to-date details of every right: **[Kol Zchut olim portal (Hebrew)](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%9D)**.

## Transferring money to Israel and banking for olim

Moving savings from abroad or paying for an apartment in Israel through a regular commercial bank can involve wide exchange-rate spreads, correspondent fees and compliance delays. It is worth comparing your bank with **regulated payment and FX companies** (licensed commercial services — not nonprofits):

- **Large transfers, absorption and real estate (payment vouchers / purchase tax / trust account):** [Adesco](/olim/moneytransfers/adesco) (payment company licensed by the Israel Securities Authority), [IsraTransfer](/olim/moneytransfers/isratransfer), [Covercy](/olim/moneytransfers/covercy)
- **Ongoing transfers, freelancers and remote work:** [Wise](/olim/moneytransfers/wise), [Payoneer](/olim/moneytransfers/payoneer)
- **Important tip:** Prepare source-of-funds documents in advance (property sale confirmation, tax returns, payslips or bank statements) — every regulated provider in Israel must verify them before releasing the transfer.
